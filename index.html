What equations drive the simulation?

Motion is horizontal at constant velocity, while gravity changes the vertical velocity. Both positions are calculated from the same time t.

Horizontal: x(t) = v₀ cos(θ) · tHorizontal acceleration is zero. Position x is measured in meters (m).
Vertical: y(t) = v₀ sin(θ) · t − ½gt²Up is positive; gravitational acceleration is −g. Height y is in meters (m).
Flight time: T = 2v₀ sin(θ) / g
Maximum height: H = [v₀ sin(θ)]² / (2g)
Range: R = (v₀² sin(2θ)) / gThe flight-time and range formulas assume the projectile lands at the same height from which it launched.
Model assumptions and units
  • Launch from (0 m, 0 m); landing at y = 0 m on level ground.
  • No air resistance, wind, thrust, or Earth curvature; point-mass projectile.
  • Initial speed v₀ in m/s; angle θ in degrees above the horizontal; gravity magnitude g in m/s².
  • For all simulation times: 0 ≤ t ≤ T; positions are in meters and time is in seconds.
